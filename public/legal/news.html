Educational note: why group savings still matter

Public encyclopedic descriptions of ROSCAs and savings groups explain that people worldwide have long pooled small regular contributions to reach goals that are hard to fund alone. In Uganda, community saving circles remain part of everyday financial life alongside Mobile Money and bank products. Digital tools can make rules and balances easier to see — they do not remove the need for trust and affordability.

Citation (informational reference): Public ROSCA/savings-group framing — paraphrased for education.

Educational note: consumer protection themes we respect

As we ship features, we try to keep product copy aligned with widely discussed consumer-protection ideas:

  • Bank of Uganda themes — understand products, protect Mobile Money credentials, beware unrealistic promises, know that supervised deposit-taking differs from informal or platform group saving.
  • World Bank financial-inclusion themes — transparency, suitability, fair treatment, and clear risk disclosure support healthier digital finance.
  • Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019 themes — purpose limitation, security, and respect for personal data.
  • URA awareness — know when activity may have tax implications; KibbiSave does not give tax advice.
  • URSB awareness — formal entity registration is separate from creating an in-app group.

These references are informational. They do not mean any regulator or institution has endorsed KibbiSave.

Safety reminder: Mobile Money hygiene

We periodically remind members:

  • Never share PINs or OTPs with anyone claiming to be KibbiSave support
  • Prefer in-app deposit flows over off-app personal transfers “to activate” an account
  • Check payee details on the provider screen before confirming
  • Report suspected fraud to your provider and, where appropriate, authorities

Read more in Responsible Saving and How deposits work.

Risk reminder (not insurance)

Like a risk-disclosure summary — not an insurance offer — we repeat that group saving involves member default risk, lock-in risk, operational risk, and fraud risk. Past leaderboard performance does not guarantee future results.

Reading tip for new members

If you are new, read in this order: About → Responsible Saving → Deposits → your specific group’s rules → then deposit. That sequence mirrors good consumer-protection practice: understand the product and risks before transferring value.

Community note: savings groups are not new

Uganda’s savings culture did not start with apps. Neighbours, churches, workplaces, and village groups have coordinated contributions for a long time. KibbiSave’s job is to make coordination clearer on a phone — with records, rules, and cautions — while staying honest that we are not a licensed bank. When we have real licensing news, it will appear here with a real identifier, not a placeholder.
